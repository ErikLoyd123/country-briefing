<Slide title="Two central banks, two very different approaches" lede="Singapore steers its currency. Vietnam steers the price and volume of credit, and the currency too.">

<Fragment slot="sg">
<BigStat value="2" unit="tightenings in 2026">
MAS raised the currency's rate of rise in April and again, by less, in July [@SG-2; @SG-3].
</BigStat>

- **No policy interest rate.** MAS manages the Singapore dollar against a secret basket of currencies, inside a band it can tilt, widen or shift [@SG-1].
- **Why tighten now:** a stronger dollar makes imported energy and food cheaper [@SG-2].
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
<BigStat value="4.5%" unit="refinancing rate">
Still at a historic low in May 2026, with the discount rate at 3.0% [@VN-1].
</BigStat>

- **More levers:** policy rates, credit-growth limits, market operations and a managed exchange-rate band.
- **The tension:** in August the government told the central bank to keep rates stable and credit flowing, while managing currency flexibly [@VN-2].
</Fragment>

</Slide>

<Slide title="What that does to your costs" lede="A strong currency makes imports cheap and people expensive. A weak one does the reverse." sgMedia="singapore/pasir-panjang-terminal.jpg" vnMedia="vietnam/saigon-river-containers.jpg">

<Fragment slot="sg">
- **Cheaper:** imported goods, energy, food and inputs [@SG-2].
- **Pricier:** Singapore-based salaries and services, priced in other currencies.
- **Currency risk:** low for an emerging-market region, but a rising dollar still squeezes margins on regional pricing [@SG-4].
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
- **Cheaper:** exports, which stay competitive as the currency slides [@VN-3].
- **Pricier:** imported equipment, energy and any debt owed in US dollars.
- **Currency risk:** build exchange-rate swings into pricing, sourcing and cash-flow plans in Ho Chi Minh City.
</Fragment>

</Slide>

<Slide title="Moving money across the border" lede="Singapore's door is open. Vietnam's opens too, but by appointment and with paperwork.">

<Fragment slot="sg">
<BigStat value="None" unit="exchange controls" />

- No restrictions on payments for trade and services, other than security-related ones [@SG-6].
- Capital and profits can be sent home freely [@SG-7].
- One reason Singapore serves as the region's treasury center.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
<BigStat value="New" unit="rules from 18 August 2026" />

- Investment money moves through a dedicated investment capital account, under a new central-bank circular [@VN-6].
- Profits can leave once a year, after audited accounts and tax finalization, and not at all while the books show accumulated losses [@VN-7].
- Choice of bank and documentation matter as much as the rules.
</Fragment>

</Slide>

<Slide title="Where the pressure sits" lede="Singapore's risks come from abroad. Vietnam's are at home." sgMedia="singapore/raffles-place.jpg" vnMedia="vietnam/saigon-skyline.jpg">

<Fragment slot="sg">
The IMF calls the system resilient and its risks contained [@SG-9]. What to watch:

- US-dollar funding and cross-border exposures [@SG-9].
- Commercial real estate and links to non-bank lenders [@SG-9].
- Cyber risk and pockets of heavily indebted companies [@SG-9].
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
Reforms are strengthening supervision, but the buffers are thin [@VN-8]. What to watch:

- Credit growing faster than deposits [@VN-9].
- Retail deposits' share falling from 48% in 2024 to 44% in 2025, with banks turning to bonds and interbank loans [@VN-9].
- Bank lending tied up in real estate.
</Fragment>

Banking, conversion and cash are reliable. The one policy risk is a stronger dollar.

<Takeaway media="vietnam/landmark-81.jpg" label="Vietnam">
The growth is real. Treat currency hedging and your choice of bank as core controls, not paperwork.
