<Fragment slot="sg">
<BigStat value="S$5,775" unit="median monthly income, 2025">
About US$4,420, up from S$3,949 in 2015 [@SG-36].
</BigStat>

- **Real wage growth:** 4.0% in 2025, about 2% a year over the decade [@SG-36]. Workers' purchasing power rose even after inflation.
- **Pattern:** steady, moderate gains that move with inflation and the economic cycle.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
<BigStat value="10.7m ₫" unit="average monthly income, 2025">
Ho Chi Minh City: about US$440, up from 7.5m ₫ in 2018 [@VN-42].
</BigStat>

- **National average:** 8.4m ₫, about US$335 [@VN-42].
- **Pattern:** steady growth, with a dip during the pandemic.
</Fragment>

<Slide title="What households spend" lede="Singapore spends on non-essentials such as personal care and health. Vietnam buys mostly essentials, with a focus on education." sgMedia="singapore/hawker-centre.jpg" vnMedia="vietnam/motorbikes-at-lights.jpg">

<Fragment slot="sg">
- **S$1,986 a month** per household member in 2023, about US$1,480, and rising since 2012/13 [@SG-38].
- **Top five, 2023:** housing, food, transport, insurance and personal care, and health [@SG-38].
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
- **2.98m ₫ a month** per person in 2024, about US$119, up from 2.16m ₫ in 2016 [@VN-44].
- **Top five, 2023:** food/groceries, housing, transport, utilities, education/development
- **Education:** spending per student rose 36.3% from 2022 to 9.5m ₫ a year in 2024 [@VN-62].
</Fragment>

</Slide>

<Slide title="Access to credit" lede="Singapore's household borrowing is modest and steady. Vietnam's credit is expanding quickly, and that brings risk.">

<Fragment slot="sg">
<BigStat value="44.1%" unit="of GDP, household debt, 2026">
Down from a peak of 66.9% in 2014 [@SG-46].
</BigStat>

- **Mature and stable:** credit is readily available to firms and households, without the rapid build-up seen elsewhere in the region.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
<BigStat value="141%" unit="of GDP, private-sector credit, 2025">
Up from 108% in 2019 [@VN-45].
</BigStat>

- **For consumers,** easier credit supports spending. **For the system,** it raises risk (see Currency & Banking).
</Fragment>

</Slide>

<Slide title="What it means for a company" layout="joined">

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
<Takeaway media="singapore/orchard-street.jpg" label="Singapore">
A high-income, high-cost, stable market that rewards quality over low prices.

<Fragment slot="detail">Incomes rise steadily [@SG-36] and purchasing power is high [@SG-37], but the city is among the world's most expensive [@SG-37]. Companies should expect a sophisticated, brand-conscious consumer base that values quality and reliability over low prices.</Fragment>
</Takeaway>
<Takeaway media="vietnam/saigon-skyline.jpg" label="Ho Chi Minh City">
A large, fast-growing market still centred on essentials.

<Fragment slot="detail">Incomes are well above the national average [@VN-42] and spending is climbing [@VN-44], but purchasing power is low [@VN-43]. Companies should anticipate strong demand growth but must tailor offerings to affordability constraints and regional income differences.</Fragment>
</Takeaway>
</div>
