## Singapore

### Transparency and corruption in practice

Third cleanest country on earth [@SG-19], and the state proves it in public: a former transport minister went to prison for twelve months in October 2024 for receiving gifts, the first political corruption trial in nearly fifty years [@SG-66]. The anti-corruption bureau charged two private-sector managers on 7 September 2026 [@SG-67].

### Trade partners and agreements

29 free trade agreements in force, including CPTPP, RCEP and the EU deal [@SG-68]. Trade was S$1.4 trillion in 2025, led by Taiwan, China, Malaysia and the US [@SG-69]. China has been the largest trading partner since 2013 and Singapore its largest foreign investor [@SG-70]; the US is Singapore's largest investor and defence partner [@SG-71]. The China-Singapore FTA was upgraded again at the end of 2024 [@SG-72].

### Civil unrest

Almost none. The 2013 Little India riot, 300 people and 62 injured, was the worst public violence in four decades [@SG-73].

### Industries most exposed to politics

- **Wealth management:** S$6.07 trillion under management [@SG-74] and a regulator that fined nine institutions S$27 million after the 2023 S$3 billion laundering case [@MC-10; @MC-11].
- **Shipping and bunkering:** record volumes in 2025 [@SG-75] and downstream of every chokepoint from Hormuz to the South China Sea.
- **Data centres and energy:** a US$330 billion build-out projected to 2050, constrained by land and power [@SG-76; @SG-95].

### What changed this week

- **8 Sep.** Political salaries raised for the first time in fifteen years; PM Wong will donate his increase [@SG-91].
- **10 Sep.** Internal Security Department keeps the terrorism threat at high; eight citizens detained or restricted since July 2025 [@SG-92].
- **Sep.** Economists lifted 2026 growth forecasts to 5 percent on AI capital spending [@SG-93].
- **23 Aug.** National Day Rally put affordability first: cheaper childcare, higher public-housing income ceilings [@SG-94].

Steady government, steady rules, and a growing question about how long a small trade hub can stay neutral between Washington and Beijing. The risk to watch is external: tariffs, chokepoints, and sanctions compliance in the banks [@SG-48; @SG-52; @MC-26].
