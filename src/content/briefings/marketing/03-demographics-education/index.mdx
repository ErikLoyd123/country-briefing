<Slide title="How educated they are" lede="More than a third of Singapore residents hold a degree. In Vietnam, most workers have no formal qualification, though the share is rising.">

<Fragment slot="sg">
<BigStat value="37.6%" unit="of residents hold a university degree, 2025">
Up from 33.0% in 2020; 64.8% have post-secondary or higher qualifications.
</BigStat>

- **Business** is the most common field of study, for men and women.
- **Multilingual:** English is the language of business; Mandarin, Malay and Tamil reach heritage audiences.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
<BigStat value="28.8%" unit="of workers have a degree or certificate, Q1 2025">
Over 70% of the workforce has no formal qualification.
</BigStat>

- **Big-city gap:** household income averages 27.5m ₫ a month in Ho Chi Minh City and 25.9m ₫ in Hanoi, against 21.9m ₫ nationally.
- **Vietnamese first:** campaigns must be in Vietnamese, outside a small urban elite.
</Fragment>

</Slide>

<Slide title="What works in a promotion" lede="An informed, older audience wants detail; a young, mobile one wants to see it and share it." sgMedia="singapore/orchard-street.jpg" vnMedia="vietnam/smartphone-chat.jpg">

<Fragment slot="sg">
- **Inform, don't shout:** specifications, comparisons, reviews and certifications persuade a degree-holding audience.
- **Two markets in one:** digital-first campaigns for under-40s; clear, trustworthy messages for a fast-growing group of seniors.
- **Premium positioning works** where the claim can be proven.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
- **Show it:** short video, demonstrations and livestreams beat long copy.
- **Two Vietnams:** aspirational, brand-led campaigns in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi; affordable pack sizes and simple messages elsewhere.
- **Grow with the middle class:** entry-level products today, trade-up ranges for tomorrow.
</Fragment>

</Slide>
