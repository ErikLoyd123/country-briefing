<Slide title="McDonald's: one brand, two stories" lede="In Singapore, McDonald's is part of the culture. In Vietnam, more than a decade in, it is still a niche." sgMedia="singapore/orchard-street.jpg" vnMedia="video/hcmc-night-intersection.mp4">

<Fragment slot="sg">
<BigStat value="153" unit="restaurants, end of 2025" size="sm">
About one for every 40,000 people [@SG-25].
</BigStat>

- **The Hello Kitty frenzy (2000):** queues of 250,000 to 300,000 people, a shattered glass door and arrests. 2.8 million toys sold, and full sets resold for up to S$980 [@SG-24].
- **Why it worked:** scarcity, collectibles and a fear of missing out.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
<BigStat value="45" unit="restaurants, end of 2025" size="sm">
Up from 37 in 2024 [@VN-33]. It is now aiming for 100 within three years [@VN-34].
</BigStat>

- **Why it stalled:** it is seen as premium, burgers are not a staple, and a bánh mì costs far less [@VN-34].
- **Who wins instead:** Lotteria (222 stores), Jollibee (213) and KFC (172) [@VN-34].
</Fragment>

</Slide>

<Slide title="Coffee: a newcomer wins Singapore, locals win Vietnam" lede="Luckin used cheap first cups and local flavours to reach 100 Singapore stores. In Vietnam, Starbucks took over a decade to reach 150, while Highlands passed 900." tone="tint">

<Fragment slot="sg">
- **Price-led entry:** Luckin Coffee arrived in March 2023 with a S$0.99 first drink for new customers [@SG-27].
- **Localised menu:** 100 stores by 2026, with a pandan coconut latte and MILO collaborations [@SG-28].
- **Its read on the market:** Singaporeans are "open to experimentation," but expect quality, convenience and value [@SG-28].
</Fragment>

<Slide title="Ride-hailing: home advantage" lede="Grab dominates its home base in Singapore. In Vietnam, an all-electric local app overtook it." sgMedia="video/sg-street-crossing.mp4" vnMedia="video/vn-motorbikes-day.mp4" split={40}>

<Fragment slot="sg">
- **Grab** is headquartered in Singapore and leads the market.
- **Its main challenger, Gojek,** has less than 10% of the market, by Maybank's estimate [@SG-29].
</Fragment>

<Slide title="Festival marketing that works" lede="Both markets buy around their festivals. Vietnam's best campaigns tell a story about going home for Tết." split={36} sgMedia="singapore/little-india.jpg" vnMedia="vietnam/motorbikes-at-lights.jpg">

<Fragment slot="sg">
- **Chinese New Year:** McDonald's brings back its Prosperity Burger as a limited-time menu [@SG-26].
- **Four calendars:** Chinese New Year, Hari Raya, Deepavali and Christmas each need their own campaign.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="vn">
- **Biti's Hunter,** a Vietnamese shoe brand, turned Tết into its brand story with "Đi để trở về" ("Leave to come home"), a 2017 music campaign with singer Soobin Hoàng Sơn [@VN-38].
- **The idea:** every journey ends at home for Tết [@VN-38].
- **It kept paying off:** the 2019 Tết film drew more than 30 million views on YouTube and Facebook and over 100,000 shares in two weeks [@VN-38].
</Fragment>

<Slide title="What the brand stories teach" layout="joined">

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
<Takeaway media="singapore/jewel-changi.jpg" label="Singapore">
Novelty, scarcity and festivals pull crowds.

<Fragment slot="detail">Limited editions, festival menus and new local flavours win attention, and a cheap first purchase wins trial. Quality, convenience and value keep the customer.</Fragment>
</Takeaway>
<Takeaway media="vietnam/coffee-meeting.jpg" label="Vietnam">
Local fit beats a global name.

<Fragment slot="detail">Local champions beat McDonald's, Starbucks and Grab on price, taste and insight. Match local habits, price for everyday budgets, and tell stories about family and Tết.</Fragment>
</Takeaway>
