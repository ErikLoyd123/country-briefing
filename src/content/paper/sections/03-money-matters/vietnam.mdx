## Vietnam

### Where the money is welcome

- Manufacturing, IT, consultancy and hotels allow 100 percent foreign ownership [@VN-57]. High-tech and large projects get 10 percent tax for 15 years, with four years tax-free and nine more at half rate [@VN-58].
- Singapore and South Korea are the top sources of 2026 investment [@VN-56]. Trade agreements with the EU, the CPTPP countries, RCEP and the UK are all in force [@VN-64].
- The 2023 Comprehensive Strategic Partnership with the US covers trade and semiconductors [@VN-65].

### Where it is not

- Twenty-five business lines are closed to foreigners and 58 are restricted; transport has 49 percent caps [@VN-57].
- Industrial parks lost their tax incentives for projects licensed after October 2025 [@VN-58]. The 15 percent global minimum tax has applied since 2024 [@VN-59].
- You do not own land. You hold use rights through allocation, lease or transfer [@VN-61].

<Callout title="Getting money out" icon="briefcase" tone="warn" size="sm">
Profits can be remitted once a year after audited accounts and tax finalisation, with seven working days' notice to the tax office. A company carrying accumulated losses cannot remit at all [@VN-60].
</Callout>

<Callout title="Seen at Crocs and Mastercard" size="sm">
Crocs planned about 47 percent of its 2025 US-bound sourcing from Vietnam [@CR-7], and it answered the tariff with three levers: shift the mix, cut cost, raise price [@CR-10]. It expected tariffs to take about 170 basis points off its third-quarter 2025 operating margin [@CR-11]. Vietnam's contactless volume grew at a 92 percent compound rate [@MC-16], and the proposed 49 percent foreign cap on e-payment firms was dropped [@MC-21].
</Callout>
