## Singapore

<Risk area="government" level="low" title="Stable to the point of predictability, with tight rules on speech">
The PAP won 87 of 97 seats in May 2025 with 65.6 percent of the vote [@SG-17; @SG-18].

The state polices public discourse: correction orders under POFMA [@SG-25], the Foreign Interference law [@SG-24], and permits for any assembly that supports or opposes a cause [@SG-26]. Foreigners need a police permit even to take part at Speakers' Corner [@SG-27].

<Fragment slot="means">You will not lose money to a coup, a strike or a policy reversal. You can lose your licence, or your visa, by treating Singapore as a place to campaign. Keep corporate voice on commercial matters.</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="government" level="low" title="Religious and racial harmony is law, not etiquette">
Speech that wounds religious feelings or promotes enmity between groups is an offence under the Maintenance of Religious Harmony Act [@SG-29].

<Fragment slot="means">Marketing, staff communications and social posts get legal review, not just brand review.</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="society" level="mid" title="Old, expensive, and getting more so">
One in five citizens is 65 or older, heading to one in four by 2030 [@SG-34]. The EIU has repeatedly named it the most expensive city in the world [@SG-35]. Housing costs only just began to cool in 2026 [@SG-36].

<Fragment slot="means">Expatriate packages are pricey and the domestic consumer base is small. Health care is excellent but co-paid; a quarter of spending is out of pocket [@SG-32]. Schools are the best in the world on PISA, which is why families come [@SG-33].</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="security" level="mid" title="Scams, not street crime" seenAt="Mastercard">
Physical crime is very low (20,857 cases in 2025) [@SG-37] while scams are the dominant risk: 37,308 cases and S$913 million lost in 2025, even after a fall [@SG-38; @SG-39].

The terrorism threat is officially assessed as high, driven by self-radicalisation [@SG-40].

<Fragment slot="means">Train staff against phishing and job scams, not muggings. Physical security is a solved problem; information security is not.</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="seen">Singapore's Payment Services Act licenses seven kinds of payment activity, so a payments company here is licensed and supervised, not just present [@MC-7; @MC-8].</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="security" level="high" title="The law is strict and it applies to you">
Drug trafficking above 15 grams of heroin carries the death penalty [@SG-41]. Vandalism carries mandatory caning; jaywalking, littering and spitting can lead to arrest [@SG-42].

Littering fines run to S$10,000 for repeat offences [@SG-43]. Flash floods are real but contained [@SG-44].

<Fragment slot="means">Brief every traveller. A visiting executive charged with a petty offence is a reputational event.</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="economy" level="high" title="A trade hub in a trade war" seenAt="Crocs">
The US put a 10 percent tariff on Singapore in April 2025 [@SG-46], and the free trade agreement did not shield exporters [@SG-47]. PM Wong called it the end of an era for rules-based trade [@SG-48].

Exports lean on China, Hong Kong, Malaysia and the US, and Coface flags exposure to a Chinese slowdown and great-power tension [@SG-52].

<Fragment slot="means">Singapore's value is as a neutral hub between the US and China. If that neutrality gets harder to hold, the hub premium shrinks.</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="seen">Crocs runs 18 company stores in Singapore and uses a third-party distribution centre here; the hub is a sales and logistics base, not a factory [@CR-17; @CR-18].</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="economy" level="mid" title="Booming on AI, dependent on imported labour">
MTI raised 2026 growth to 4.5 to 5.5 percent in August 2026 on the AI capital spending wave [@SG-49; @SG-50]. The workforce runs on 1.6 million foreign workers [@SG-53].

Coface notes dependence on imported energy and food and labour and housing shortages [@SG-51].

<Fragment slot="means">Hiring is competitive and pass-dependent. Energy and land constrain the data-centre boom you may be counting on.</Fragment>
</Risk>
