## Vietnam

<Risk area="government" level="high" title="One party, a lot of change, and a purge that reaches the top">
The anti-corruption campaign forced out a president and four Politburo members in 2024 [@VN-24]. In 2025 it disciplined 23 centrally managed officials and charged 46 local leaders [@VN-23].

The state cut provinces from 63 to 34 [@VN-21] and ministries from 30 to 22, with 100,000 public jobs going [@VN-22].

<Fragment slot="means">Your government counterpart may change. Your permit may sit in a merged province with a new boss. Build relationships with the institution, not one official, and expect delay while officials avoid signing anything that could be questioned [@VN-66].</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="government" level="high" title="Data lives here now" seenAt="Mastercard">
Decree 53 lets the Ministry of Public Security order foreign digital providers to store user data in Vietnam for at least 24 months [@VN-25]. The Personal Data Protection Law took effect 1 January 2026 with fines up to 5 percent of revenue for cross-border transfer violations [@VN-26].

The updated cybersecurity law (July 2026) requires content removal within 24 hours and local storage of user data [@VN-28].

<Fragment slot="means">Anything with a login, a payment or a customer database needs a Vietnam data plan before launch.</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="seen">Decree 53 requires foreign online-payment providers to store specified data in Vietnam and open a branch or representative office; Mastercard's own filings list data-localisation mandates as a business risk [@MC-22; @MC-28].</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="government" level="low" title="The reform is real too">
The amended Law on Investment, effective March 2026, cut 38 conditional business lines and lets a foreign investor set up a company before the investment certificate [@VN-29].

<Fragment slot="means">Entry is getting easier on paper. Test it in practice on the November visits.</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="society" level="mid" title="Young, willing, and under-credentialed">
The labour force is 52.9 million and grows by 500,000 a year, but only 28.8 percent hold a diploma or certificate [@VN-33]. PISA scores fell sharply from 2018 [@VN-32].

The World Bank warns Vietnam risks getting old before it gets rich [@VN-34].

<Fragment slot="means">Labour is available and cheap; skilled supervisors are not. Budget for training.</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="society" level="mid" title="You will breathe the air">
Hanoi was the 10th most polluted major city on 15 January 2026 [@VN-30], and Vietnam ranks 18th most polluted country [@VN-31]. Local hospitals do not accept US insurance [@VN-9].

<Fragment slot="means">Expatriate health is a retention issue in Hanoi especially. Base senior staff with care and insure for evacuation.</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="security" level="high" title="Weather is the disaster risk">
Typhoon Yagi killed 318 people and did US$3.3 billion of damage in September 2024 [@VN-36]. The year 2025 set the national rainfall record and flooded the south-central coast, killing 158 [@VN-38].

Ho Chi Minh City set rainfall and tidal records in 2025 [@VN-39]. Kajiki cut power to 1.6 million customers in August 2025 [@VN-37].

<Fragment slot="means">Factories and warehouses need flood and power contingency, and the season runs June to November, exactly when we visit [@VN-7].</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="security" level="mid" title="The road is the crime">
There were 8,515 traffic deaths in the first ten months of 2025 [@VN-35]. Bag snatching from motorbikes and taxi meter scams are the main urban risks; use established firms like Mai Linh and Vinasun [@VN-41].

<Fragment slot="means">Ban staff from motorbike rentals [@VN-10] and put a car policy in the travel rules.</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="economy" level="high" title="A tariff aimed at the business model" seenAt="Crocs">
The US tariff is 20 percent on most Vietnamese goods and 40 percent on anything judged transshipped from China [@VN-43]. The October 2025 framework kept the 20 percent while Vietnam dropped tariffs on almost all US goods [@VN-44].

The US is Vietnam's biggest export market at US$153 billion, with a US$134 billion surplus [@VN-45], and 40 percent of Vietnam's imports come from China [@VN-46].

<Fragment slot="means">The whole "China plus one" strategy runs through here. If your product cannot prove Vietnamese origin, the 40 percent rate is your problem.</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="seen">Vietnam made 45 percent of Crocs Brand shoes in 2025 and one manufacturer with most of its operations in Vietnam makes nearly half [@CR-1; @CR-3]. Crocs told investors a 10 percent tariff on its sourcing countries costs about US$45 million a year [@CR-8].</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="economy" level="mid" title="Fast growth, rising prices, a weak dong">
Growth was 8.02 percent in 2025 and the target is 10 percent for 2026 [@VN-48]. Inflation hit 4.89 percent in August 2026 [@VN-49].

The dong lost about 3.5 percent officially and nearly 7 percent on the street in 2025 [@VN-51]. Power cuts cost US$1.4 billion in 2023 [@VN-50].

<Fragment slot="means">Price in dong depreciation and plan for outages. The upside is real: the World Bank sees the region's fastest growth [@VN-54].</Fragment>
</Risk>

<Risk area="economy" level="mid" title="Finance can still surprise">
Vietnam's largest fraud, US$12.5 billion at SCB, ended with a death sentence in 2024 [@VN-52]. More than US$7.7 billion of corporate bonds mature in 2026, half from property developers [@VN-53].

Coface calls the banking system fragile [@VN-20].

<Fragment slot="means">Know your bank. Prefer the large state and foreign-linked banks for treasury.</Fragment>
</Risk>
