Two companies we will visit in November, followed across the border. What each does in each country, the political risk it hit, what it did, and what changed when it crossed.

<Callout title="Crocs: what changed at the border" size="sm">
In Singapore Crocs faces a small tariff and no ownership or currency friction. In Vietnam it faces the tariff that was designed around its supply chain, and moving away is hard because every alternative country carries a similar rate [@CR-4]. The lesson for a smaller entrant: the sourcing country is now a political choice, and the mitigation is diversification you build before you need it. Vietnam's footwear exports still grew 5 percent in 2025 [@CR-22], and the EU agreement is phasing footwear tariffs to zero [@CR-23].
</Callout>

<Callout title="Mastercard: what changed at the border" size="sm">
In Singapore Mastercard is a licensed innovator under a regulator that publishes the rules and enforces them. In Vietnam it is a partner to state institutions, because the state owns the switch, sets the data rules, and can propose an ownership cap in a draft decree. The lesson: in Singapore compliance is your cost; in Vietnam relationship is your licence [@MC-13; @MC-21; @MC-22].
</Callout>
