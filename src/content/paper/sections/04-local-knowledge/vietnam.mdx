## Vietnam

### Crocs: the factory floor

Crocs owns no factories [@CR-24]. The two brands went opposite ways: Vietnam's share of Crocs Brand shoes fell from 56 percent in 2023 to 45 percent in 2025 [@CR-1], while HEYDUDE moved from 5 percent Vietnam to 44 percent as it left China [@CR-2; @CR-25]. One manufacturer with most of its operations in Vietnam makes nearly half of all Crocs Brand product [@CR-3].

**The risk it hit.** The July 2025 US framework: 20 percent on Vietnamese goods, 40 percent on transshipment [@CR-21]. Crocs' own filing lists 20 percent on Vietnam and similar rates on every alternative [@CR-4]. The company warned tariffs raised costs and that further price rises could hurt demand, with a disproportionate hit to HEYDUDE [@CR-5; @CR-6].

**What it did.** Three levers: sourcing mix, cost cuts, price [@CR-10]. Cost: about US$45 million a year per 10 percent of tariff [@CR-8], and roughly 170 basis points of quarterly operating margin in late 2025 [@CR-11]. Revenue fell 1.5 percent to US$4.04 billion [@CR-12]. International Crocs Brand sales still grew 11.9 percent [@CR-14].

### Mastercard: partner to the state

A representative office in Ho Chi Minh City since 2008 [@MC-12]. In 2024 it launched Vietnam's first co-badged card with NAPAS, the national switch, through nine banks [@MC-13], pairing NAPAS' 600,000 acceptance points with global acceptance [@MC-14]. It supplies contactless ticketing for Metro Line 1 with FPT IS [@MC-15].

**The risk it hit.** Rules that rewrite themselves. Decree 52 (July 2024) defined e-money and gave banks 24 months to comply on international payment systems [@MC-19; @MC-20]. Decision 2345 requires biometrics on transfers above 10 million dong [@MC-18]. Decree 53 requires local data storage and a local office [@MC-22]. A proposed 49 percent foreign ownership cap on e-payment firms was dropped after industry pushback [@MC-21].

**What it did.** Partnered with the state rather than around it: the NAPAS card, the metro, a 2026 to 2030 tourism agreement with the national tourism authority [@MC-29], and a new country manager in September 2025 [@MC-31]. The prize: cashless payment value reached 28 times GDP in 2025 [@MC-23], and contactless grew at a 92 percent compound rate [@MC-16].
