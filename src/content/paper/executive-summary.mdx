Doing business in Singapore and Vietnam, told the way a guidebook tells it: what to know before you land, what can go wrong, what it means for your money, and what to ask when you are standing in the room. Two companies we will visit in November, Crocs and Mastercard, run through every section so you can watch the same firm cross the border.

## Singapore: the next 12 months (our read)

Steady government, steady rules, and a growing question about how long a small trade hub can stay neutral between Washington and Beijing. The risk to watch is external: tariffs, chokepoints, and sanctions compliance in the banks [@SG-48; @SG-52; @MC-26].

## Vietnam: the next 12 months (our read)

A consolidated leadership pushing for 10 percent growth, an investor upgrade landing this month, and a tariff that punishes exactly the transshipment model that built the export boom. Expect fast rule changes on data and payments, and expect the courtesy of delay from officials who would rather not sign [@VN-75; @VN-91; @VN-43; @VN-66].

## What changes at the border

**Money.** In Singapore, profits and capital move freely, with no withholding on dividends [@SG-60; @SG-61]. In Vietnam, profits can be remitted once a year after audited accounts and tax finalisation, and a company carrying accumulated losses cannot remit at all [@VN-60]. This is the single biggest practical difference between the two.

**Crocs.** The sourcing country is now a political choice, and the mitigation is diversification you build before you need it.

**Mastercard.** In Singapore compliance is your cost; in Vietnam relationship is your licence [@MC-13; @MC-21; @MC-22].
